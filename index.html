How WinchForce works

  

    
Using it

    
    
      
  • Set the deck area you've measured, then lay out the line from the winch to the end. Drag points on the plan or type their positions. Height is above the deck.
    • 
      
  • Under What do you know? enter one reading. It can be the winch pull, the load at the end, a load cell under a block (side load), or an inline load cell in one leg. Everything else is worked out from it.
    • 
      
  • Each point has a rigging stack from the catalogue, for example a snatch block, its shackle and the padeye. Every item is checked against the load on that point and the weakest one governs.
    • 
      
  • Green is under 80 % of the limit, yellow is 80–100 %, red is over.
    • 
    

    
Friction at a bollard or chock

    
The capstan equation, using the angle the line actually turns through at the point, plus any full turns taken around it. A line running straight past turns 0° and loses nothing.

    T_out = T_in · e^(−μ · θ)      θ = turn angle + 360° × extra turns
    
Sheaves and blocks

    
Each sheave the line runs over costs a fixed efficiency η, typically 0.95–0.98 depending on the bearing.

    T_out = T_in · η
    
When heaving in, friction works against the winch, so the line gets lighter toward the load. When paying out it reverses and the load side is the heavier one.

    
Load on a point

    
The vector sum of the two legs pulling on it, in 3D, so heights give uplift or down-load as well as side load. A block with the line turning back on itself carries close to twice the line tension, which is why a load cell under a sheave can read double the line.

    R = T_in · û(to previous point) + T_out · û(to next point)
    
Purchases

    
For an n-part purchase each part loses one sheave efficiency from the hauling part.

    Hook load W = T × (1 + η + η² + … + ηⁿ⁻¹)  (heaving)
    
Limits

    
  • Lines are taken as straight between points. Line weight and stretch are ignored.
  • A load cell reading under a block includes the block's own weight; subtract it if it matters.
  • This is a planning aid. Final rigging must be checked against certified equipment and your company procedures.
