How WinchForce works

  

    
Three steps

    
    
      
  • Lay out the line. Tap the winch (W) or the end (E), then tap where it is on the grid. Add bollards, blocks or fairleads with + and place them the same way.
    • 
      
  • Say what you know. The winch pull, the load at the end, or a load cell reading. Tap the grid to show where the load cell is.
    • 
      
  • Check it's safe. Add the limits you know: winch max pull, the wire, the SWL of each point. Anything over its limit turns red. Tap a point or a line to see its load.
    • 
      
  • Bollards. In More, choose a bare post or a snatch block on top, and its foot height. You get the side pull, the up/down pull and the bending at the foot (side pull × lever).
    • 
      
  • Placing precisely. Tap a point: the loads clear and a crosshair shows how far it is from every other point. Tap the arrows to nudge it (hold to repeat), tap the ± tag to change the step, or tap anywhere on the grid to put it there. Tap the point again or Done to finish. Dragging only moves the view.
    • 
      
  • Grid size. Double-tap an empty part of the grid, then drag the corner. Double-tap again to finish.
    • 
    

    
Friction at a bollard or fairlead

    
The capstan equation on the angle the line turns through, plus any extra full turns. A line running straight past turns 0° and loses nothing.

    T_out = T_in · e^(−μ · θ)
    
Blocks and sheaves

    
Each sheave costs a fixed efficiency η, typically 0.95–0.98.

    T_out = T_in · η
    
Heaving in, the line gets lighter toward the load. Paying out, it reverses.

    
Load on a point

    
The two legs pulling on it added together, in 3D. A block with the line turning right back carries close to twice the line tension, which is why a load cell under a sheave can read double.

    
Purchases

    Hook load = line tension × (1 + η + η² + … ) for each part
    
Limits

    
  • Lines are straight between points. Line weight is ignored.
  • A load cell under a block also carries the block's weight.
  • Planning aid only: check against certified equipment and your procedures.
